    Context

    The music catalog market in 2026.

    Independent artists and producers are increasingly recognizing the long-term value locked in their catalog. A music catalog — recorded masters, publishing rights, or both — represents years of creative work that continues to generate royalty income long after a release date. In 2026, the secondary market for established catalogs has expanded well beyond major-label acquisitions to include independent creators across every genre, from ambient and lo-fi to hip-hop, R&B, and electronic music.

    SPACE buys revenue-generating music catalogs directly. Unlike auction platforms or listing marketplaces, SPACE is a direct buyer: one review, one point of contact, no commission taken from any offer. Artists, producers, independent labels, and catalog owners can apply regardless of genre or catalog size. Every application is reviewed individually based on documented income, transferable rights, and the streaming trajectory of the catalog.

    What makes a catalog worth reviewing

    Catalog acquisitions depend on documented income, transferable rights, and the trajectory of your streaming data. The most common catalogs SPACE reviews include independent artists with consistent streaming income across Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music, and similar platforms; producers who own masters outright and can document royalty history from distributors such as DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby, or direct label deals; independent labels with a defined catalog of releases and artist agreements that clearly specify rights ownership; and composers with sync licensing income from placements in film, television, advertising, or games.

    A catalog review does not require a minimum track count or a guaranteed earnings threshold. SPACE reviews each application based on the income documentation and rights you can provide. Missing information may require follow-up; no application guarantees an offer.

    Recording rights versus publishing rights

    Understanding the difference between recording rights and publishing rights matters before you apply. Masters cover the specific recorded performance of a song. Publishing rights cover the underlying composition — melody, lyrics, and arrangement. You may own one, both, or neither, depending on your distribution and label agreements. When you apply, SPACE asks about both. If you have only one set of rights to offer, that is worth discussing; the offer, if made, will reflect what is actually transferable. Confirm what you own with your distributor, PRO, or a music lawyer before submitting.

    Partial sales and phased exits

    You do not have to sell everything at once. SPACE accepts proposals for selected tracks, a defined share of rights — such as 50%, 75%, or 100% — or a full catalog transfer. Artists who want to retain a creative connection to their work while accessing a lump-sum payment sometimes choose a partial sale. The review is designed to help you understand your options before committing to anything. No obligation is created by starting a review; you can step back at any point before signing an agreement.

    What happens after the review

    If a catalog is a fit, SPACE follows up privately to confirm the income documents and rights evidence needed to finalize the review. An initial offer, if made, typically follows within 5 to 7 business days after the relevant information is received. You review the terms — ideally with an independent music lawyer — before deciding whether to proceed. Payment follows the completed agreement and satisfied rights-transfer conditions. Nothing is automatic; every step requires your active decision. There is no obligation to accept an offer once it is made.

    Questions

    Everything you want to know.

    What kind of music catalogs does SPACE buy?

    We consider revenue-generating catalogs across all genres, including hip-hop, R&B, pop, rock, ambient, meditation, lo-fi, focus, wellness, nature soundscapes, electronic, house, techno, trance and drum & bass. Artists, producers and labels can apply. Documented income and transferable rights require individual review; no genre guarantees an offer.

    How does the process work?

    Start with the guided catalog review on our Apply page. We review the information you submit and follow up privately for any additional evidence. An offer depends on catalog fit, documented income and rights. Payment requires a completed agreement and satisfied transfer conditions.

    Can I sell only part of my catalog?

    You can propose selected tracks or a share of defined rights, such as 50%, 75% or 100%, subject to ownership and agreement. Recording and publishing rights are separate. Confirm exactly what transfers and what you retain.

    What do I need to qualify?

    We review whether we can contact you, which rights you can transfer and what income your statements document. There is no automatic qualification decision from a calculator result or a stated minimum on this page. Missing information may require follow-up; an application does not guarantee an offer.

    Do I lose creative control?

    Control depends on the agreement's scope, approvals and continuing obligations. Identify the existing recordings and works being sold, and confirm any provisions affecting future releases with an independent music lawyer.

    How is my catalog valued?

    We review documented net income, trends, platform and track concentration, rights and contracts. The reviewer confirms the history needed. No fixed genre multiple or guaranteed price is supplied by the calculator; it only models assumptions you choose.

    How long does the process take?

    If the catalog is a fit, an initial offer typically follows within 5-7 business days after the relevant information is received. Missing evidence or complex rights can require more time. Payment follows the completed agreement and satisfied transfer conditions.

    What payment methods do you support?

    The existing offer policy provides for bank transfer after the agreement is complete and rights-transfer conditions are satisfied. Confirm payment instructions, currency and any deductions in the agreement.
