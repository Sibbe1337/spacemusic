# SPACE: multilingual SEO and competitor opportunities

**Research date:** 2026-09-16. **Status:** recommendations only; no site edits or deployment.

Three research agents investigated native-language intent, competitors/content
gaps, and international SEO architecture. The parent checked authenticated Search
Console and Google Keyword Planner, then reconciled their findings here.

## Decision

**Improve the English commercial journey first. Then test one supported language,
not five translated websites.** Germany has the clearest numeric signal among
the non-English phrases sampled, but it is small: 10 monthly searches for
`musikkatalog verkaufen` and 20 for `musikrechte verkaufen`. This is not proof
that Germany's entire catalog-sale market is larger than Spain's or Brazil's.

The larger immediate gap is credibility and intent matching on existing pages.
There are already 141 sitemap URLs, yet approximately 84% of recorded Google
clicks go to the calculator. More genre pages or bulk translation would not, by
themselves, solve that concentration or substantiate old commercial claims.

## Recommendations: priorities and content gaps

### 1. Correct existing claims before translating them

The current release still contains claims such as cinematic ambient commanding
the highest multiples, sleep/wellness sector-size headlines implying an acquisition
opportunity, and an old Royalty Exchange comparison saying SPACE is exclusively
ambient/lo-fi/wellness. The last claim contradicts the current all-genre position.
The competitor FAQ also contradicts unsupported partial-sale and universal-fee
simplifications. Sources: [Sell](https://spacemusic.io/sell/),
[existing comparison](https://spacemusic.io/compare/space-vs-royalty-exchange/),
[Royalty Exchange FAQ](https://royaltyexchange.com/resources/creators/frequently-asked-questions-about-selling-royalties).

Refresh these URLs in place with sourced, conditional explanations. Do not delete
useful pages or promise that changes cannot affect rankings. Align HTML and the
client-rendered copy. The pending blog design preview does not resolve these facts.

### 2. Make the existing Sell page answer the seller's decision

Use the page to explain: what interest the owner can sell; selected tracks versus
a full catalog; ownership/split evidence; existing agreements; what happens to
future music; the review stages; and what a free review does not guarantee.
Genre exploration is secondary to these decisions. Approval is needed for a
title/H1 or policy change; this research supplies no new acceptance threshold.

The calculator already attracts most traffic. Keep the scenario limitations and
connect it naturally to the valuation explanation, documents and free review.
Audit page-filtered non-brand queries before revising snippets. A new competing
valuation article is not needed.

### 3. Strengthen the label journey, not another generic funding article

The [label funding article](https://spacemusic.io/blog/independent-record-label-funding-options/)
already exists. Improve the path from that article to the
[independent-owner page](https://spacemusic.io/for/independent-artist-catalog-owner/)
and Apply. Explain the label's own net interest after artist/producer shares,
assignment restrictions, selected existing releases and retained obligations.

A distinct `/for/independent-record-label/` commercial page is a conditional
candidate, not an automatic publication: it must have a real label workflow,
verified service boundaries and a non-duplicative purpose. Do not advertise
SPACE grants, debt or advances to capture the broader funding query.

### 4. Replace unverified proof with real evidence

The existing case-study URL and several old author biographies remain unverified.
The user has explicitly confirmed that the legacy named contributors are not
verified. Do not amplify these claims in translated pages or structured data.
Review the current [case article](https://spacemusic.io/blog/case-study-ambient-producer-catalog-sale/)
against records and permission; otherwise clearly distinguish a hypothetical
teaching example from a real transaction after approval.

Useful new evidence would show actual rights sold, retained shares, the income
period, conditions, documented process and seller permission. A genuine non-mood
music example could support broader positioning; an invented one cannot.

### 5. Pilot one genuinely supported language

| Market | Recommendation | Evidence and uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| English / US first | First content and conversion priority | Strongest measured seller-query demand and current click source; UK/Canada can benefit from accurate English pages but their keyword volumes were not checked. |
| German / Germany | First candidate for a small SEO pilot, conditional on service and human review | Two seller phrases have reported volumes, plus current German GSC visibility. Still only a small measured niche. |
| Spanish / Spain and Mexico | Validate as an alternate service/conversion pilot | Real Spanish commercial pages exist; SPACE has a prior Spanish-language inquiry in the user-provided context. That is not a country demand estimate, a sales conversion or proof of support capacity. Sampled phrases had no reportable volume. |
| French / France and Portuguese / Brazil | Hold broad rollout; reassess with actual inquiries and expanded keyword evidence | Native commercial vocabulary/providers exist, but tested phrases supplied no numeric demand estimate. |
| Swedish / Sweden | Only a low-cost service choice if support is easy; not a measured SEO growth bet | Six current country clicks do not establish Swedish-language demand; all four sampled Swedish phrases returned a dash. |

Do not build both Germany and Spanish pilots simultaneously merely to hedge.
Confirm operational language support first; it can override the small numerical
difference in the sampled demand. Language research findings:
[native intent report](/Users/emilsoujeh/spacemusic/marketing/multilingual-research-20260916/languages/report.md).

## Safe multilingual implementation, after approval

Preserve all **141 existing sitemap URLs** and leave English at its current paths.
For a German pilot, a possible commercial URL is `/de/musikkatalog-verkaufen/`.
Start with one reviewed seller page and the minimum process, preparation and
Apply context needed to complete the journey, not 38 genre translations.

Each translation needs its own self-canonical, correct document language and
reciprocal hreflang counterparts generated from one reviewed manifest. No forced
IP/language redirects, invented offices, local legal expertise or unsupported
country eligibility. [Google's localized-page guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions).

The current site mounts English React content over static HTML, and its marketing
source checkout is incomplete. Copying translated HTML while keeping the same
entry could overwrite language, metadata or routing. Use a reproducible isolated
static pilot, or restore the source before a broader locale-aware implementation.
Test raw HTML and JavaScript-on/off versions, canonical/hreflang reciprocity,
mobile layout, deep links, form fallback, errors and source-path attribution.

Typeform language entitlement and published translations need verification. Keep
the existing integration/question IDs and notification contract unless an approved
change is tested. Translate notices, validation and follow-up expectations too;
otherwise disclose the English form/correspondence before the CTA. Never imply an
end-to-end translated service when only the landing page is translated.

Detailed implementation gates and evidence:
[architecture brief](/Users/emilsoujeh/spacemusic/marketing/multilingual-research-20260916/architecture/BRIEF.md).

## GEO and follow-up

For Google AI search, prioritize crawlable, accurate service facts, useful original
evidence and indexed pages. Google explicitly says `llms.txt` does not affect its
visibility/rankings; it is not the growth lever suggested by the earlier automated
audit. This Google-specific statement does not establish behavior of every AI
provider. [Google AI optimization guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

No ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity mention/citation scan was measured in this task.
A future fixed-prompt panel should separate languages and ask about selling a
partial catalog, independent-label funding via existing rights, required documents,
direct buyers versus marketplaces, and what happens to future music. Record exact
prompt, engine, date, location if controlled, response and cited URLs; do not claim
coverage from an unrun test.

Recommended sequence: (1) approved factual corrections and clearer Sell journey;
(2) existing label/valuation pathways and verified proof; (3) preview-only locale
pilot; (4) approved production release with rollback; (5) review after equal complete
28- and 56-day periods. Keep country, device, page and query definitions fixed.
Track index selection and non-brand clicks separately from completed applications,
manually qualified applications and closed transactions. Low sample sizes may
remain inconclusive; report counts and uncertainty, not a promised lift.

## Limits and deliverables

No deployment, outreach, paid-tool purchase, campaign change or production form
submission occurred. Account access was read-only. Keyword rows were transcribed
from the UI; the blocked CSV export was not bypassed. Native-language web searches
were not controlled country-local Google rank snapshots. No SEO difficulty,
competitor backlink gap, supported-country matrix, language-level conversion rate
or AI citation lift was verified. No ranking or traffic guarantee is made.
