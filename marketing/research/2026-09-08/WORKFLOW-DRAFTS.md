## Draft 1: Partial Sale vs. Full Sale: How Much of Your Catalog Should You Sell?

Destination: https://spacemusic.io/compare/partial-sale-vs-full-sale/

Keep the existing H1 and search title. This draft changes the explanation, not the address.

### Start with what “your catalog” includes

If you want upfront capital without treating every right you own as one asset, first define the interest you are considering selling. A partial sale and a full sale are not meaningful comparisons until the recordings or works, territories, income sources and ownership shares are the same.

Selling all of your interest in ten named recordings is not automatically a sale of your publishing, future releases or every royalty you receive. In the US, copyright ownership can be transferred in whole or in part; the actual scope and applicable law still need professional review. [US Copyright Office, Section 201](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap2.html#201).

### Prepare a scope before comparing prices

Use one schedule for the assets and a second for the earnings attributable to the interest you control. Record your owned percentage, the proposed percentage of that interest to sell, exclusions, existing commitments and any permissions that remain unresolved.

Bring the relevant agreements and original statements, not just access to a distributor account. The [seller's document checklist](https://spacemusic.io/blog/music-catalog-sale-documents-checklist/) explains how to organize evidence privately. Preparing a scope does not establish that the rights are transferable or that SPACE will make an offer.

### Worked example: half of your share is not half of everything

Suppose you own 60% of the defined recording rights. The remaining 40% belongs to someone else. Your documented net income attributable to your 60% is $600 a month, and you propose selling half of your own interest.

| Item | Illustrative result |
| --- | --- |
| Your interest before the proposed sale | 60% of the defined recording rights |
| Interest offered | 50% of your 60%, or 30% of the whole |
| Your retained interest | 30% of the whole |
| Other owner's interest | 40%; not included in your proposal |
| Scenario using an arbitrary multiple of 4 | $600 × 12 × 4 × 50% = $14,400 |

The 4 is not a market multiple or a SPACE offer. Do not apply the 60% again to the $600: this example's income already represents your owned interest. The example assumes the proposed scope can be transferred and does not resolve consent, costs, accounting or tax questions.

### Compare the rights you keep, not just the price

Ask who will collect revenue, how retained receipts are calculated, which deductions apply and what statements you receive. Ask separately about licensing approvals, distribution decisions, credit, reporting and what happens if the buyer transfers its interest later. Keeping a percentage does not by itself answer those contract questions.

A full sale of the specified interest may still leave contractual duties. A partial sale does not guarantee growth, a better total outcome or the ability to repurchase the sold interest. Use the [offer checklist](https://spacemusic.io/blog/music-catalog-offer-checklist/) with an independent music lawyer to review the actual clauses, including any buyback or reversion provision.

### Next step

SPACE considers proposed sales of defined revenue-generating rights across genres, subject to ownership and individual review. There is no published universal percentage that you should sell. [Request a free catalog review](https://spacemusic.io/apply/); you do not need to choose a valuation multiple in the initial application. Review and payment conditions are explained in the [offer policy](https://spacemusic.io/pricing.md).

**Scope:** educational comparison from a prospective buyer, not independent legal, investment or tax advice. Hypothetical numbers are not evidence of eligibility or a completed sale.

**Legacy consistency checklist:** replace the current “almost always better,” “20–40%,” expected wellness-market growth and guaranteed-control statements. Keep partial/full trade-offs, price comparison and buyback questions, answered conditionally rather than as universal advice. Check table cells and FAQ JSON-LD as well as paragraphs.

## Draft 2: Selling Masters vs. Selling Publishing: Understanding Your Two Assets

Destination: https://spacemusic.io/compare/selling-masters-vs-selling-publishing/

Keep the existing H1 and search title. Do not create another “masters versus publishing” article.

### Separate the assets before adding the income

If you record and write music, an offer for “your catalog” may leave a crucial question unanswered: does it concern particular recordings, interests in the underlying compositions, a defined royalty stream, or a combination?

The US Copyright Office distinguishes a sound recording from its underlying musical composition. Ownership of one does not establish ownership of the other. A recording may also contain material for which you do not control the composition rights. [Copyright Office Circular 56A](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ56a.pdf).

### Build two schedules

For the recording schedule, list the recordings, identifiers, supported ownership shares, relevant agreements and income sources. For the composition schedule, list the works, writers, supported shares and relevant publishing or administration agreements. Identify what the proposed deal excludes.

Match each receipt to the correct schedule. A distributor statement, publisher statement and collection-society statement should not be treated as interchangeable evidence of the same asset. Spotify explains that it pays rights holders and that contracts affect the subsequent payments to artists and songwriters. [Spotify's payment explanation](https://loudandclear.byspotify.com/faqs/how-do-artists-and-songwriters-get-paid/).

An ISRC helps identify a recording, but is not a substitute for ownership evidence. ISWC identifies a musical work and does not specify copyright ownership shares. [IFPI](https://isrc.ifpi.org/why-use-isrc/ownership), [ISWC](https://www.iswc.org/iswc).

### Worked example: a recording-only proposal

Imagine the following documented interests and income. These are invented figures, not industry averages.

| Schedule | Supported ownership in this example | Monthly net income attributable to your interest | Proposed transaction |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Named recordings | 100% | $800 | Sell 50% of that defined interest |
| Related compositions | 50% | $200 | Excluded from this proposal |

An illustrative recording-only scenario using an arbitrary multiple of 4 is $800 × 12 × 4 × 50% = $19,200. The composition income is not added to a recording-only calculation. Its $200 already represents the seller's interest, so it should not be reduced by 50% again when describing that existing income.

This arithmetic does not say what either asset is worth. It also does not establish how later receipts, costs or approvals would work under an agreement. Those are separate review questions.

### Check what the agreement would change

Ask your adviser which rights transfer and which remain, including any limits concerning licensing, adaptations, new recordings, distribution or future work. Do not assume that retaining a composition interest automatically permits every new recording or use; existing agreements and other rights may matter.

No genre determines that masters or publishing must be the better asset to sell. Compare actual scope, documented income, retained interests and obligations rather than a general claim that one asset is always more valuable or more “evergreen.”

Use the [document checklist](https://spacemusic.io/blog/music-catalog-sale-documents-checklist/) to collect the evidence. Keep the [valuation guide](https://spacemusic.io/guides/music-catalog-valuation-2026/) for the method rather than importing a fixed multiple into this comparison.

### Next step

[Request a free catalog review](https://spacemusic.io/apply/) if you want SPACE to assess a defined proposal. Explain what you control when the reviewer requests the supporting information; the initial form does not require uploading both schedules. SPACE's published review process is not a promise to acquire every type of publishing or recording interest. See the [offer policy](https://spacemusic.io/pricing.md).

**Scope:** a practical preparation workflow, not legal advice on ownership or a recommendation to sell either asset. US legal references do not establish another country's law.

**Legacy consistency checklist:** correct the absolute opening “Every piece of recorded music creates two...” and remove unsupported genre-specific superiority, universal control and guaranteed future-income claims. Retain questions about selling both assets and comparative value; answer them using defined scope and individual assessment rather than a universal ranking.

## Draft 3: Catalog Sale vs. Catalog Loan: Compare Rights, Repayment and Cash

Destination: https://spacemusic.io/compare/catalog-sale-vs-catalog-loan/

**Recommended title/H1 change:** replace “Catalog Sale vs. Catalog Loan: Monetizing Your Music Without Losing It.” That wording implies ownership cannot be at risk and describes a sale as if it retained the asset. Proposed search title: `Catalog Sale vs. Catalog Loan: Rights & Repayment | SPACE`. Keep the URL. Inspect the current meta description at implementation; change it only if it repeats the same misleading assurance.

### The same cash amount can describe very different arrangements

If you need upfront money, first ask what you would exchange for it. A proposal to acquire defined rights, a loan secured against assets, and an advance recouped from specified revenue should not be compared on their headline amounts alone.

SPACE describes a direct acquisition process, not a catalog-loan product. Funding providers may offer other structures: beatBread, for example, describes customizable artist funding and a review using distribution data. The provider's written terms, not its product label, must establish the obligations in a specific offer. [SPACE policy](https://spacemusic.io/pricing.md), [beatBread artist funding](https://www.beatbread.com/artist-funding).

### Obtain the terms before building the comparison

Ask each counterparty to identify the assets or income included, money available at closing, fees and deductions, duration, collection arrangements and the conditions for payment. Have an independent adviser identify any repayment obligations, security, guarantees, recoupment provisions and consequences if income falls.

For a proposed sale, ask what you retain and which duties continue after payment. For a loan, ask for the repayment schedule and the remedies available if obligations are not met. For an advance, ask which revenue can be collected, how recoupment is calculated, and whether any obligations remain at the end of the stated term. Do not assume “advance” means either a conventional loan or a permanent rights sale.

### Worked example: compare actual cash, then the obligations

These invented offers illustrate why the first number is insufficient. They are not SPACE or third-party terms.

| Written item | Illustrative purchase proposal A | Illustrative purchase proposal B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Headline consideration | $30,000 | $30,000 |
| Amount dependent on a future condition | $5,000 | $0 |
| Disclosed balance settled at closing | $2,000 | $2,000 |
| Cash at closing before other costs and taxes | $23,000 | $28,000 |

Even these two purchase proposals cannot be ranked without matching their rights, share and duration. An advance or loan offering $30,000 upfront adds another comparison: what subsequent receipts or payments are committed, under what conditions, and for how long?

Use the written terms to model a lower-income case as well as your expected case. For illustration only, if $1,000 of monthly income fell by 40%, it would become $600. Ask what your actual contract would require in that situation; this page does not assume repayments adjust automatically or recommend a borrowing ratio.

### Limits that matter before you commit

Do not use an annual-income valuation multiple as a loan duration or a predicted repayment period. Do not assume a full sale removes every future duty, or that a financing arrangement makes rights, distributions or retained income risk-free. Tax treatment, enforceability and acceptable terms require independent advice for your jurisdiction and circumstances.

Keep a dated comparison sheet and use the [offer checklist](https://spacemusic.io/blog/music-catalog-offer-checklist/) to identify missing terms. For the named provider comparison, continue to the existing [beatBread versus catalog buyers article](https://spacemusic.io/blog/beatbread-vs-catalog-buyers/); this page does not reproduce its provider-specific details.

That is not an application for a loan, a promise of an offer, or independent financial advice. Confirm subsequent terms against the [published offer policy](https://spacemusic.io/pricing.md) and the actual written proposal.
