### Voiceover transcript

How much could your music catalog sell for? A calculator can show a scenario, but it cannot tell you what a buyer will offer.

Start by defining the rights and income you control. Recording rights and composition rights are different, and a proposed sale may cover only selected tracks or part of your interest.

Use documented monthly net income for that scope. A distributor receipt may already have fees deducted. Do not subtract the same cost twice, and keep the reporting period and currency consistent.

Here is an invented example: one thousand dollars a month, multiplied by twelve, an assumed multiple of four, and a fifty-percent sale. That gives twenty-four thousand dollars. Four is an arbitrary assumption, not a market benchmark or a SPACE offer. The result is not your after-tax cash at closing.

If you do not know a multiple, do not guess just to finish the form. You can leave it blank and request a review instead. The valuation guide explains the factors beyond this arithmetic.

Before review, organize the evidence: original royalty statements, the rights you own, relevant agreements and any unresolved questions. The document checklist helps you keep those records separate and private.

SPACE's initial application asks for an email and artist or label name. You do not need to upload everything to make first contact. Relevant supporting information follows privately by email.

The initial review is free and carries no obligation to accept. If you receive a proposal, compare the rights, payment conditions and remaining duties with independent advice. Requesting a review is the next step, not a commitment to sell.
